# Certified bounded homogeneous search

## Claim boundary

The completed 4A calculation determines `B4((4A)^4,2500)` exactly: it consists
of three pure components of 2,456 inner classes each. Every other generating
inner class lies in a component with a replayable connected witness of 2,501
classes, so it is outside the bound.

The analogous 5A statement would be the exact determination of
`B4((5A)^4,30400)`. It would require every generating inner class to be
assigned to a closed component of size at most the bound or to a replayable
connected witness of size `bound + 1`. The repository completed the 5A outer
pair-orbit partition and symbolic macroblock census, but not the global
traversal, so no 5A bounded-exhaustiveness claim is made.

## Exact decomposition

Write a homogeneous tuple as two factor pairs:

```text
(s1,s2,s3,s4),  p = s1*s2,  s3*s4 = p^-1.
```

For a target class `C`, simultaneous-conjugacy orbits of ordered pairs in
`C x C` are indexed by the double cosets
`C_G(c) backslash G / C_G(c)`. Each pair orbit is normalized so its product is
the chosen representative of one M23 conjugacy class.

For each product class `P`, a macroblock is a Cartesian pair of:

- one normalized pair orbit with product in `P`;
- one normalized pair orbit with product in `P^-1`.

The macroblock identifier is
`(natural_product_class, left_pair_id, right_pair_id)`. IDs are ordered
lexicographically after the normalized pair representatives are sorted. The
full macroblock set is stored as 17 Cartesian ranges rather than expanded rows.

Inside one macroblock, let `H=C_G(p)` and let `S_left,S_right <= H` stabilize
the two factor pairs. With the right pair written as a conjugate by `h`, the
remaining inner Nielsen classes are represented exactly by
`S_right backslash H / S_left`. This final relative double-coset step
materializes product-one inner classes without scanning `C^3`.

## Coverage certificates

The pair checkpoint records every normalized pair orbit and its stabilizer.
The independent verifier:

- checks both entries lie in the target class;
- checks the fixed product representative;
- reconstructs every `C_G(p)` orbit and rejects overlap;
- recomputes each pair stabilizer;
- proves the sum `|G|/|stabilizer|` equals `|C|^2`;
- verifies the Cartesian macroblock ranges cover their recorded total.

The compact character-table census independently partitions the complete
normalized structure constant by product class using class-multiplication
coefficients. These weights include nongenerating tuples and can be rational
when nongenerating stabilizers are nontrivial; they are an outer checksum, not
the final generating-class count.

## Bounded traversal

For each generating inner-class seed, deterministic fixed-first pure-braid BFS
has two terminal states:

- `closed`: the complete component closes at or below the bound;
- `overflow`: exactly `bound + 1` distinct connected classes have been found.

The seed and deterministic algorithm are sufficient to replay an overflow
witness independently. The 4A regression closes a translated component at
2,456 classes and produces 2,501 distinct classes from an ambient macroblock,
exercising both outcomes at bound 2,500.

## Current scale

| Target | Pair orbits | Symbolic macroblocks | Pair checkpoint |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4A | 10,099 | 7,928,185 | about 2.8 MB |
| 5A | 45,344 | 161,177,452 | about 12 MB |

The macroblock count is the scaling problem. Processing must be streaming,
deterministically sharded, and able to reuse known closed/overflow component
certificates; writing 161 million database rows before doing mathematics would
discard much of the symbolic compression.

### Ledger pilot

A first streaming pilot used a class-level ledger: each new seed performed a
capped traversal, and later traversals stopped if they touched any ledger class.
On ten adjacent 4A/order-11 macroblocks (110 generating classes), this still
required 12 traversals: nine new 2,501-class overflow witnesses and only three
links, taking about 75 seconds. Extrapolation is plainly unacceptable.

The class-level ledger is retained as a diagnostic scanner, not the production
coverage algorithm. The next implementation must make the pair-twist orbit
inside each macroblock an exact graph vertex and traverse the resulting block
quotient. For cyclic product centralizers generated by the product element
(orders 8, 11, 14, 15, and 23), the relative-centralizer double cosets already
give this block directly. Noncyclic centralizers require an explicit refinement
before their macroblocks can be treated as vertices without merging distinct
pure components.

A tuple-to-macroblock locator and finite pair-twist refinement now establish
this vertex definition. For a sampled order-11 macroblock, the two remaining
pure generators lead to 22 neighboring macroblocks. Fourteen are already exact
cyclic blocks. The remaining samples refine as expected: order-5 blocks split
into three orbits of size 5, order-7 into two of size 7, an order-4 block into
eight of size 4, and an order-6 block remains one orbit of size 6.

The production coordinate prototype now avoids repeated whole-tuple
canonicalization. For every nonidentity product class it stores transporter
lookups inside the comparatively small product centralizer, locates a tuple in
its relative double coset, and computes pair-twist blocks directly. Regression
checks cover product orders 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11, 14, 15, and 23 and compare
the cyclic cases with the independent canonical implementation.

The identity-product macroblock is necessarily different: its product
centralizer is all of M23. It is represented instead by the 10,099 target-pair
double cosets. Its pair twists are singleton quotient vertices. The direct
locator covers this block without constructing a transporter table for M23.

Pair checkpoint schema 2 retains the original fixed second entry and the
transporter that normalized each pair product. This doubles the 4A checkpoint
from about 1.4 MB to 2.8 MB, but lets a braid edge use its explicit first-entry
conjugator and a target-centralizer lookup. Schema-1 checkpoints remain readable
through a slower reconstructed lookup.

The two remaining pure generators now give exact edges between refined
vertices. A weighted quotient traversal closes the known 4A component at 484
vertices representing exactly 2,456 inner classes. The first implementation
took about 124 seconds after locator construction because it normalized each
edge with a whole-group `RepresentativeAction`. Reusing the stored pair
normalizers reduces the same exact traversal to about 14.2 seconds, an 8.7-fold
speedup. This is now a practical component engine, though the complete
7.9-million-macroblock scan still needs persistent coverage scheduling.

### Resumable quotient scan pilot

A deterministic scanner now persists refined vertices and three certificate
types: `closed`, `overflow`, and `linked`. It atomically checkpoints after each
macroblock, resumes without repeating completed blocks, and emits dashboard
events. A separate verifier replays every certificate against the prior ledger
and then proves that every generating refined vertex in every scanned
macroblock occurs in the ledger.

On ten adjacent order-11 4A macroblocks, all ten seeds produced independent
overflow certificates. They covered 2,507 refined vertices in roughly 32
seconds of incremental scanning, and the complete replay took about 26 seconds.
This is a large speed improvement over the earlier class-level pilot, but zero
links in ten sequential blocks shows that ordinal locality does not match graph
locality. The next scheduling refinement should process macroblocks reached by
new certificates as a priority frontier before resuming the ordinal stream.

### Frontier and dual-threshold pilots

The frontier scheduler persists both its priority queue and a rotating ordinal
cursor. Among its first 100 nonidentity 4A macroblocks, 99 of 213 generating
vertices were ledger hits and 67 macroblocks needed no new certificate. The
23,078 stored vertices comprise 78 overflow and 36 linked certificates. This is
substantially better reuse than adjacent ordinal order, and the independent
verifier exactly replays the frontier processing order.

It is not yet fast enough. The 100-block run took about five and a half minutes,
and dense noncyclic macroblocks can expose dozens of new refined vertices at
once. At 792 KB after 100 blocks, atomic whole-record checkpoint replacement is
also unsuitable for millions of blocks.

The scanner now separates the mathematical proof limit from a larger optional
coverage-walk limit. With proof limit 2,500 and walk limit 25,000, a component
that closes between the limits is still classified as larger than the bound;
otherwise the larger witness offers more opportunities to link. A 20-block
exploratory run stored 12,533 vertices in 11 certificates, eight linked, and
grew the reusable ledger about three times faster per second than minimal
overflow walks in this small comparison. This parameter needs a larger
benchmark after persistence ceases to dominate.

### Transactional database ledger

The scalable persistence prototype keeps certificates, certificate vertices,
frontier ordinals, processed blocks, and the active crash-recovery lease in
SQLite. GAP remains responsible for all group and braid calculations and talks
to a persistent Python ledger process over buffered pipes. Certificate insertion
and vertex ownership are one transaction, with a unique `(macroblock,twist)`
key. Restart returns an unfinished active block rather than advancing the
frontier, so certificates committed before interruption are reused safely.

The database reproduces the ten-block in-memory pilot exactly: three
certificates, seven fully covered blocks, nine hits, and 765 vertices. Its fair
single-process runtime was about 14 seconds, roughly 15 percent slower than the
in-memory run. Resuming to 20 blocks produced 11 certificates and 2,796 vertices
in a 228 KB database. The database verifier independently replays certificates
in order and reconciles all processed-block coverage and counters.

Whole-record checkpoint rewrite cost is therefore removed without changing the
mathematical result. The current service deliberately grants only one active
block lease; safe parallel leases and conflict handling come after larger
single-worker walk-limit benchmarks.

### Matched walk-limit and worker benchmarks

On the same first 20 frontier blocks with proof bound 2,500:

| Walk limit | Vertices | Seconds | Vertices/sec | Overflow | Linked |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2,500 | 2,796 | 41 | 68.2 | 11 | 0 |
| 10,000 | 7,698 | 106 | 72.6 | 5 | 6 |
| 25,000 | 12,533 | 141 | 88.9 | 3 | 8 |

The 25,000 walk limit is provisionally best, growing reusable certified coverage
about 30 percent faster than minimal walks. This is still a small sample and
must be checked beyond the first dense frontier region.

SQLite now supports renewable multi-worker leases and atomic certificate
conflict detection. A wavefront barrier makes workers wait when no committed
frontier exists but another lease is active, avoiding premature unrelated
ordinal seeds. Two workers correctly reproduced the 20-block 25,000-limit state
and exact replay, but took about 150 seconds rather than the single worker's 141
seconds. Equal block budgets load-balance poorly because covered blocks are
cheap and dense traversals are expensive. Parallelism is retained as a safe
prototype, not enabled as the production default.

### Sustained 100-block result

The single-worker 25,000-limit database run now covers 100 nonidentity frontier
macroblocks. It contains 47,102 vertices in 114 certificates, of which 110 are
linked and four overflow. Total scanning took about 464 seconds across a
20-block invocation and an 80-block resume, for 101.5 ledger vertices/second.
The resumed portion alone reached about 107/second. The comparable minimal-walk
100-block pilot achieved roughly 70/second, so the larger coverage walk retains
about a 45 percent advantage beyond the initial sample.

The 3.0 MB database passes SQLite integrity checking and exact replay of all
114 certificates. Replay took about 434 seconds. At this scale the principal
cost remains GAP traversal, not indexed persistence.

Multi-worker scheduling now uses a transactional global block target rather
than equal local quotas. A balanced two-worker 20-block run handled a genuine
certificate conflict correctly and replayed to the same 12,533 vertices, but
improved elapsed time only from about 141 to 136 seconds. This marginal gain
does not justify making block concurrency the default.

### Replayed 10,000-block base

The production frontier ledger now contains 10,000 processed blocks and
597,298 vertices in 18,593 certificates. All but seven certificates link into
prior coverage: 18,586 linked, four overflow, and three closed. The 9,000-block
extension ran at 1.67 blocks/second, about 3.6 times the preceding 900-block
rate, and complete replay reconstructed the ledger exactly. The verified 40 MB
database is frozen as a read-only SHA-256-named base for parallel experiments.

Deterministic virtual shards own block ordinal `n` by
`1 + ((n - first_ordinal) mod shard_count)`. Worker leases, committed block
rows, and the audit manifest record this assignment. Physical workers claim
available shard transactions dynamically to avoid dense-traversal tails and merge online
through short SQLite transactions and unique vertex ownership; this avoids the
ambiguous overlapping certificates that independent writable delta ledgers
would otherwise require GAP to retraverse during merge. The auditor checks the
base checksum and row preservation, exact ownership of every delta block, no
active leases, database integrity, the declared block target, and a final
certificate-order replay.

An eight-worker 9,000-block wave from an exactly replayed 11,000-block base
completed in 767 seconds, or 11.73 blocks/second, and produced an audited
20,000-block database with 817,722 vertices. All 14,221 delta certificates
linked into prior coverage. At this sustained rate, complete 4A block coverage
projects to about 7.8 days.

Replay can be composed from contiguous certificate-ID segments: each segment
loads the exact ownership prefix it depends on, replays its certificates, and
reports status and vertex deltas. Independent block segments rematerialize
their assigned blocks and recount generating vertices and ledger hits. A final
auditor checks that the segments cover the whole delta and that their sums equal
the database differences. Four-way replay improved 447 seconds to 146 seconds;
the larger 9,000-block delta passed eight-way certificate and four-way block
verification in 748 seconds. Four replay workers alongside eight scan workers
are the provisional 32 GB production setting: a matched four-way replay took
1,154 seconds, equivalent to 7.80 verified blocks/second and an approximately
11.8-day complete projection. Eight replay workers improve the projection to
about 7.6 days but need more memory headroom for safe overlap.

The next production wave confirms the 32 GB balance at larger scale. Eight
workers scanned 40,000 blocks in 3,978 seconds (10.055/second), while four
certificate and four block segments replayed the same delta in 3,907 seconds
(10.238 verified blocks/second). The audited 50,000-block ledger contains
1,237,281 vertices in 77,938 certificates. Since replay is slightly faster than
scan, pipelined waves should not accumulate a verification backlog. The slower
scan rate projects complete 4A enumeration in about 9.1 days.

The first candidate-snapshot pipeline then extended the verified base to
100,000 blocks while preserving a simultaneous speculative extension to
150,000. The 50,000-block candidate delta scanned in 5,149 seconds (9.711/sec)
and passed four-certificate/four-block replay in 3,545 seconds (14.104 verified
blocks/sec). The immutable 100,000-block base contains 1,666,133 vertices and
140,184 certificates; all 62,246 delta certificates linked to prior coverage.
The concurrent 100,000-to-150,000 scan completed in 6,875 seconds (7.273/sec)
with 1,984,032 vertices and 197,992 certificates. That live boundary is now
being verified while scanning advances to 200,000.

That next wave was interrupted when a mistakenly overlapping full regression
suite exhausted memory and the kernel killed all scan and certificate replay
workers. Transactional restart retained 151,572 committed live blocks, replayed
the frozen 150,000-block candidate exactly, and resumed to 200,000 without
database repair. The promoted 150,000-block base contains 1,984,032 vertices in
197,992 certificates. The live 200,000-block boundary contains 2,254,107
vertices in 253,831 certificates and passes integrity checking with zero leases.
The incident validates crash recovery but excludes the contaminated interval as
a clean throughput benchmark.

### Complete 4A result

The deterministic production waves scanned all 7,928,184 nonidentity
macroblocks and passed exact certificate-order replay. A final audit then
detected that this was not yet exhaustive: of the 10,099 singleton quotient
vertices in the exceptional identity-product macroblock, 4,976 are generating
and only 500 were already owned. The 4,476 missing generating vertices were
therefore scanned explicitly rather than treated as an implied consequence of
the nonidentity run.

Every one of those 4,476 added certificates linked to prior coverage. The
complete immutable ledger consequently has:

- 7,928,185 processed macroblocks;
- 5,349,177 certificates and 10,826,112 owned quotient vertices;
- three closed, four overflow, and 5,349,170 linked certificates; and
- all 4,976 generating identity vertices owned, with no nongenerating identity
  vertex owned.

The identity delta passed an independent exact certificate-order replay. A
separate GAP audit reconstructed the identity block from the pair checkpoint,
retested generation directly in `M23`, and checked ledger ownership vertex by
vertex. It then materialized the seeds of closed certificates 781, 1396, and
15731 and ran three uncontracted fixed-first pure-braid enumerations. They are
pairwise disjoint and each closes at exactly 2,456 inner classes. The final
read-only database has SHA-256
`c638c0aa0fe4918b2c86c0ea446e8591fd250ee0de21ba12cab570cf523a6835`.
Test 0054 gives commands, inputs, versions, and provenance.

## Publication

The 4A result is packaged separately for review at
<https://github.com/GLitchfield/m23_4a_bounded_proof>: proof inputs, the final
ledger manifest and audit results, independent checking programs, and a Lean
development. The 1.2-GiB operational ledger is not part of either
repository's source tree; the spin-off offers it as a release download.

MapClass was used as a comparison implementation and a source for exact
generating-tuple accounting ideas. Its standard full-orbit algorithm is not
used as the bounded coverage engine, and no GPL-licensed source is copied into
this MIT-licensed project.
